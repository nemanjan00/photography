**Belgrade, 25 October 2026.** The night SNS fell.

Almost two years after the Novi Sad station canopy collapsed and killed sixteen
people, the students who took to the streets over it won at the ballot box:
the highest turnout since 1992, a projected majority for the Student List, and
Aleksandar Vučić conceding before the night was out.

Since 2012, SNS was Serbia's return to the '90s — the people who led us into
war, stained our history and killed our neighbours, back in a new, almost as
evil form. Rule of crime, rule of money, rule of the cheap; rule of people who
never learned to be ashamed.

That night Ćacilend, their hooligan camp outside the Assembly, came down, and
people carried its chairs home as trophies. A beautiful night, full of hope.

End of lies. End of blackmail. End of shame. End of fear.
**Start of freedom.**

Election day was just incredible. It gave us so much hope — in the first few
hours turnout was double that of previous elections, and we just felt
happiness. I voted in Belgrade, and in the whole queue I didn't hear a single
person say they supported SNS. I did see homeless people being brought to vote
in unmarked cars, which told me they were stealing votes, again.

Then night came and the polling stations closed. Then silence, for about an
hour. Then I saw photos of SNS people getting ready to celebrate in Niš, and my
dreams came crashing down. Should I move out of Serbia?

Five minutes later, Vučić went on TV and admitted defeat.

I was in shock at first. Then came an immense happiness —
finally freed of the evil that was done to all Serbian people, and to my
family. Then relief that it was over, after such a long fight and an especially
intense week. I'm not ashamed to say I let a few tears out. Tears of joy.

I set off for the National Assembly and nothing stopped me. Nobody cared about
red lights, nobody cared where we were going — everyone just went where they
felt they should. We had finally won. I wanted to hug the whole world; I felt
all the power in it.

And then the joy of reaching Ćacilend. I came expecting thousands of hooligans
behind that fence — and the fence was gone. Happy people were walking out of
there, smiling, holding white Ćacilend chairs over their heads like they had
won the world.
