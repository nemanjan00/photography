**Belgrade, 25 October 2026.** The night SNS fell.

Almost two years after the Novi Sad station canopy collapsed and killed sixteen
people, the students who took to the streets over it won at the ballot box:
the highest turnout since 1992, a projected majority for the Student List, and
Aleksandar Vučić conceding before the night was out.

End of lies. End of blackmail. End of shame. End of fear.
**Start of freedom.**

<!-- more -->

Since 2012, SNS was Serbia's return to the '90s — the people who led us into
war, stained our history and killed our neighbours, back in a new, almost as
evil form. Rule of crime, rule of money, rule of the cheap; rule of people who
never learned to be ashamed.

Election day was incredible. In the first few hours turnout was double the last
election's, and all we felt was hope. In the queue in Belgrade I didn't hear a
single person speak for SNS — but I did watch unmarked cars bring homeless
people in to vote. They were stealing votes, again.

The polls closed. Then an hour of silence. Then photos of SNS people getting
ready to celebrate in Niš, and my dreams came crashing down. Should I leave
Serbia?

Five minutes later, Vučić went on TV and admitted defeat.

First, shock. Then a happiness I can't describe — free, at last, of the evil
done to all Serbian people, and to my family. Then relief, after such a long
fight and an especially hard week. I'm not ashamed to say I cried. Tears of joy.

I set off for the Assembly and nothing could stop me. Nobody cared about red
lights, nobody cared where we were going — everyone just went where they felt
they should. We had won. I wanted to hug the whole world.
